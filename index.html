Who we are

A community-owned organisation for voices too often unheard.

Safe Space Community Center (SSCC) is built by and for the people it serves ”” independent, gender-neutral, and rooted in Kireka Kamuli.

Too many men carry grief, parenthood and financial pressure alone ”” in a society that rarely encourages them to ask for help.

SSCC stands with widowers, single fathers, young fathers and other marginalised people ”” providing a place to be heard, a network of peers, and practical routes to economic independence.

A place to be heard. A network that understands. A route to independence.

Our community

Our vision

A community where every marginalised individual ”” regardless of gender, family status or circumstance ”” has a voice, a support network, and the opportunity to thrive emotionally and economically.

Our mission

To advocate for and uplift marginalised people ”” through emotional support, challenging harmful norms, impactful campaigns, and economic projects that build financial independence.

Our objectives

What we are working toward.

  1. 01

    Provide safe spaces and peer-support for widowers, single fathers, young fathers and other marginalised people.

  2. 02

    Challenge harmful social norms that discourage men and other marginalised groups from seeking support.

  3. 03

    Run at least two structured community campaigns each year to raise awareness and mobilise resources.

  4. 04

    Establish income-generating and economic empowerment projects that grow members' financial capacity.

  5. 05

    Build a well-governed, gender-balanced organisation meeting national CBO standards.

  6. 06

    Form partnerships with local leaders, government services and other organisations.

Governance

Open, accountable, member-led.

SSCC is governed by its members through the General Assembly and managed day to day by an elected, gender-balanced Executive Committee.

How we”’re governed

General Assembly
All members
Executive Committee
Elected, gender-balanced
Term
2 years, renewable once
Leadership
5 founding ’’ 10 at full strength
Elections
Simple majority
Ask a governance question

It starts with five founding members and grows to ten. Executive members serve a two-year term, renewable once, elected by simple majority.

  • ChairpersonOverall leadership; presides over meetings; bank co-signatory.
  • Vice ChairpersonDeputises for the Chairperson; supports programme coordination.
  • SecretaryConvenes meetings, keeps minutes and records; handles correspondence.
  • TreasurerManages funds; presents financial reports; bank co-signatory.
  • Committee member / mobiliserMobilises members and the community.
  • Projects coordinatorOversees campaigns and economic projects.
  • Publicity / communicationsManages public image and communication.
  • Community representativesRepresent beneficiary groups and the wider community.

  • Annual General Meeting ”” once every year, with 14 days”’ notice.
  • Ordinary General Assembly ”” every quarter.
  • Executive Committee ”” every month.
  • Special meetings ”” on 48 hours”’ notice when urgent matters arise.
  • Quorum ”” a simple majority of members; resolutions passed without quorum are not binding.

  • Sources of funds ”” membership fees, donations, grants, and project proceeds.
  • Bank account ”” held in the organisation”’s name with at least three signatories; every transaction needs two signatures.
  • Reporting ”” the Treasurer presents a financial report at every General Assembly.
  • Audit ”” accounts reviewed at least once a year.
  • Spending controls ”” expenditure above a threshold requires prior approval; no personal use of funds.

  • Treat one another with dignity, respect and confidentiality.
  • Attend meetings, pay contributions, and take part in campaigns and projects.
  • Protect the organisation”’s name, property and resources.

Prohibited: political or religious campaigning, discrimination, misuse of funds, disclosure of personal stories without consent, harassment, illegal activity, and substance abuse. Members facing expulsion receive written notice and a fair hearing, with appeal rights within 30 days.
